Local SEO

Your Google Business Profile is doing more work than your website

A complete guide to why your Google Business Profile often drives more local visibility than your website — and exactly what to fix first.

Updated for 2026 · 9 min read

For a lot of local businesses, the Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is the first — and sometimes only — thing a potential customer sees before deciding whether to call, visit, or scroll past. When someone searches for a business like yours nearby, the results they see first are usually a map, a cluster of star ratings, and a short list of details pulled directly from your Business Profile. Your website might not appear at all until the searcher scrolls past the Local Pack, or clicks through from your listing. If that profile is thin, outdated, or missing entirely, you're losing the first impression before a visitor ever reaches your homepage.

This isn't a minor technical detail — it's often the single highest-leverage piece of local SEO a small or medium business can control, and it typically takes far less time to fix than a website redesign.

Why Google Business Profile matters more than most businesses realise

Local search behaviour has shifted heavily toward "near me" and map-based queries. When someone searches "plumber near me" or "coffee shop Benoni," Google prioritises the Local Pack — the block of three business listings with a map — above standard organic website results for the vast majority of local, service-area, and storefront searches. That Local Pack is built almost entirely from Business Profile data: your category, your hours, your review score, your photos, and how recently you've posted an update.

In other words, a searcher can form a complete impression of your business, compare you to two competitors, and make a decision — all without ever leaving Google's results page. That makes your profile, not your homepage, the actual first page of your website for a large share of local traffic.

What a complete Google Business Profile actually includes

A fully built-out profile goes well beyond a name, address and phone number (often called NAP data). To get real value from it, make sure the following are all filled in and kept current:

  • Accurate opening hours — including holiday hours and temporary closures, since incorrect hours are one of the fastest ways to lose a customer who shows up to a closed door.
  • The right primary category, plus any relevant secondary categories, since category selection directly affects which searches you appear for.
  • A clear, specific services or products list rather than a generic description.
  • Regularly added photos — interior, exterior, team, and product photos all perform better than stock imagery, and profiles with recent photos tend to get more clicks.
  • Short posts about offers, events, or updates, published at least a few times a month.
  • Answered questions — Google lets anyone ask a question directly on your profile, and it stays visible to everyone until someone (ideally you) answers it.

Reviews do more than build trust — they influence rankings directly

Reviews are often treated as a vanity metric, but they influence both whether someone chooses you and how visible you are in local search results in the first place. Google's local ranking algorithm factors in review count, average rating, and recency — a business with a steady flow of recent, responded-to reviews signals to Google that it's active, trusted, and relevant right now.

A steady flow of reviews — and replies to them, good or bad — signals that a business is responsive. Ignoring reviews, especially negative ones, leaves an impression that nothing has changed since the complaint was posted, which can be more damaging long-term than the original review itself. A short, professional, non-defensive reply to a negative review is often enough to reassure future customers that the issue was handled.

The most common gaps we see in Google Business Profiles

The same handful of issues show up again and again when we audit a new client's profile:

  • Hours that don't reflect a recent change in trading times.
  • A photo gallery that hasn't been updated in years, or is dominated by low-quality or irrelevant images.
  • Unanswered questions sitting at the top of the profile for months.
  • Duplicate listings, created by accident over time, that split reviews across two profiles and confuse both customers and search engines.
  • A category selection that no longer matches what the business actually offers.

None of these are difficult to fix on their own — the challenge is usually finding the time to do it consistently, month after month, alongside everything else involved in running a business.

A simple monthly checklist to keep your profile healthy

If you do nothing else, running through this short list once a month will keep most local SEO problems from ever building up:

  • Confirm your hours are correct, including any upcoming public holidays.
  • Add at least one new photo.
  • Reply to every new review, positive or negative, within a few days.
  • Check for and answer any new questions.
  • Publish one short update post — an offer, a new product, or a recent project.

Google Business Profile and your website work together, not instead of each other

A well-maintained Google Business Profile won't replace a good website, but it often does more of the day-to-day work of getting found, building trust, and turning a search into a visit or a call. Your website remains essential for deeper information, conversions, and broader organic search visibility beyond local intent — but for the specific moment someone searches for a business like yours nearby, your profile is doing most of the heavy lifting.

If your profile hasn't had attention in a while, it's usually one of the fastest, lowest-cost improvements available to a local business — often showing measurable results within weeks rather than the months a website or SEO campaign can take.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I update my Google Business Profile?

Aim to check your profile at least monthly: confirm hours are correct (especially around holidays), add any new photos, and respond to new reviews and questions within a few days. Businesses that post updates or offers weekly tend to see more engagement from Google's local search results.

Does Google Business Profile really affect SEO rankings?

Yes. Your Business Profile is a primary ranking factor for the Local Pack and Google Maps results, which sit above organic website listings for most local searches. Profile completeness, review volume and recency, and consistent business information all directly influence local ranking.

How do I respond to a negative review?

Respond promptly, acknowledge the specific concern without being defensive, and offer to resolve the issue offline where possible. A calm, professional reply is visible to every future visitor and often does more to build trust than the negative review does to damage it.

What's the difference between a Google Business Profile and a website for local SEO?

Your Business Profile appears directly in Google Maps and the Local Pack, often before a searcher visits your website, and is weighted heavily for "near me" searches. Your website supports broader SEO and conversions — for immediate local visibility, the two work together.
